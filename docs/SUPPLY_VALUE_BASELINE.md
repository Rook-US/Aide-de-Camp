# Supply stock amounts and percentage baselines (game/save 1.142)

Local investigation, 8 October 2026. The installed `Assembly-CSharp.dll`, `Config/campaignprefs.txt`, `Config/unitprefs.txt`, and the player's game-written G-campaign save `Save7_10_2026_22_35_17` are the primary evidence. The [read-only baseline audit](../scripts/Audit-SupplyBaseline.ps1) matches each combat record to its `paths.dat` record using the game's four-field path identity and fully parses the counted records. It found 526 matched combat units in that save: 294 infantry, 72 cavalry, and 160 artillery. These observations do not yet validate a production supply write or another game version.

## What one saved stock unit means

Each `paths.dat` combat record has four float `supplyinstock` amounts in order: small-arms ammunition, artillery ammunition, provisions, forage. The numbers are **normalized game stock units**, not displayed percentages, physical rounds, or rations. The installed `Regiment.Initialize` sets all four amounts to current active `strength` when no stock array exists. `BattleUnits.CreateUnitDefault` also sets the relevant ammunition and provision/forage slots to active strength. Thus the game-established initial/full baseline for a new unit is **one stock unit per active soldier per slot**. Examples from the player's game resave: a new 1,000-man infantry has `(999.1487,1000,1000,1000)` after slight consumption; a new 600-man cavalry has `(599.4893,600,600,600)`; a new 60-man artillery battalion has `(60,59.94893,60,60)`.

**1,000 is not a universal stock ceiling or a pool shared by categories.** The same 1.142 audit against the game-written A-campaign save `Save10_4_2025_0_38_9` found infantry and cavalry units with 2,000 small-arms stock and 2,000 provisions stock (and cavalry with 2,000 forage stock). The G-campaign's inspected combat units top out at 1,000 in their relevant slots, matching their smaller unit sizes. Each category is stored separately. A 1,000-active-soldier infantry at full initial stock is therefore approximately **1,000 small-arms stock plus 1,000 provisions stock**, not 1,000 split between them. Its saved artillery slot may also contain 1,000 as a placeholder, but infantry does not use that slot as artillery ammunition.

The inactive ammunition slot can still contain a strength-sized value. For example, the 1,000-man infantry has 1,000 in the artillery slot and the 60-man artillery has 60 in the small-arms slot. The game's ammunition branch selects slot 0 for infantry/cavalry and slot 1 for artillery. These inactive-slot values must **not** be presented as physical ammunition carried, or independently treated as a meaningful supply category for that branch.

In a [developer explanation of campaign and battle ammunition](https://steamcommunity.com/app/654890/discussions/4/3731827719872593100/), Captain Müller describes units carrying ammunition and the top supply group carrying reserves. The installed `Config/unitprefs.txt` has separate group-reserve durations (1 day for each ammunition type, 2.5 days for provisions and forage). Those reserve days are distinct from the four per-unit stock amounts; they are not a conversion into physical rounds or rations.

## Two denominators in the installed game code

`Regiment.GetAvgSupplyState` divides applicable stock by **active strength** (minimum divisor one). That is the unit's own stock-ratio basis. For infantry it averages small-arms ammunition and provisions; for cavalry and artillery it uses the branch's ammunition plus provisions and forage. Setting every slot to `active strength × 0.5` therefore targets a 50% unit stock ratio.

Campaign `Regiment.UpdateIIPSupplyLink` calculates an amount to replenish against **active strength + sick + wounded** for the relevant slot. In the saved combat record, `Total_Men = active strength + sick` and `Wounded = losses[0]`, so this campaign refill target is `Total_Men + Wounded`. The `I. CORPS` artillery-store figure in two paired game saves was exactly the mean of subordinate artillery stock divided by that same `Total_Men + Wounded` denominator, to saved precision. Battlefield `BattleUnits.ResupplyAllUnits` instead uses active strength in its refill calculation. These code paths make a single universal stock percentage misleading for units with sick or wounded men.

The saved 1st US Cavalry illustrates the difference: 656 active, three sick, 659 total men, zero wounded. Campaign refill target is 659. Its provisions stock is 659, which is 100% of the campaign target but 100.46% of active strength. The [audit script](../scripts/Audit-SupplyBaseline.ps1) reports both denominators and raw values. Percentages in ADC should show the chosen basis and preserve the exact raw value; values above 100% of active strength can be legitimate and must not be silently clipped.

## Editor implication and remaining game test

A percentage editor can convert a requested percentage into a stock amount by multiplying a **named, validated denominator**. Raw-value mode can expose the exact float saved in `paths.dat`. The UI should distinguish a unit's stock percentage from a command's aggregate store icon and from its separate incoming-flow percentage. A percentage/raw mode switch is a presentation and input preference; it cannot alter the saved meaning of the four amounts. For relevant ammunition, use the unit type; preserve the inactive ammunition slot without implying the unit consumes it.

## Installed configuration and the apparent cap

The installed `Config/unitprefs.txt` defines **Maximum Unit Size** by unit type: currently 1,000 for infantry, 1,000 for cavalry, and 240 for artillery. `BattleUnits.CreateUnitDefault` calls `GetMaxUnitSize` for a new unit unless given a size override, then initializes supply amounts from the resulting strength. This is an **indirect way unit-size configuration changes the normal stock baseline**. It is not an independent per-category supply-cap setting. The game-written A-campaign save with 2,000-stock infantry/cavalry records shows that 1,000 is not intrinsic to the save format; it may reflect a different unit-size configuration when that save was made.

The other installed supply settings govern different things. `Config/unitprefs.txt` sets top-command battle reserves in days (1 for each ammunition type; 2.5 for provisions and forage). `Config/campaignprefs.txt` specifies supply-to-goods ratios, supply/refill speed, consumption time, and modifiers. A search of the installed text configuration did not find a separate combat-unit `supplyinstock` maximum or stock-per-soldier multiplier. In the inspected assembly, `Regiment.Initialize` directly copies `strength` into each initial stock slot, while campaign replenishment compares stock against `strength + sick + wounded`. Neither code path reads a separate stock-cap preference. Therefore changing maximum unit size would affect many unit mechanics and is not an appropriate ADC control for changing supply capacity alone.

[Test 9](DIVISION_SUPPLY_STOCK_EXPERIMENT.md) targeted 50% of active strength in all four slots for 18 units under 1st Infantry Division. The player's paused game resave retained all 72 amounts exactly and recalculated the division's store icons to 49.84532%, 50%, 49.88828%, and 49.96748%. After time advanced, the four icons rose to 56.96682%, 56.24148%, 73.27759%, and 70.75156%. This validates existing-unit stock writes for this observed 1.142 hierarchy and demonstrates the percentage conversion's approximate relationship to HQ display. The exact per-tick replenishment and consumption amounts vary with campaign conditions and have not been mapped to a stable editor setting. New-unit stock records remain unvalidated.
