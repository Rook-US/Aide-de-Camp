# Campaign supply hierarchy in the installed 1.142 game

Read-only evidence, 8 October 2026. Source: the installed `Grand Tactician Manual.pdf` (pages 90 and 113–114), the installed `Assembly-CSharp.dll`, and the player's game-written G-campaign save `Save7_10_2026_22_35_17`. The observations below apply to that installed build and save version; they do not define a supported ADC supply write.

## What the campaign panel shows

The manual describes the selectable army, corps, and garrison unit panel. It distinguishes **Supplies**, an incoming supply-flow percentage relative to current consumption, from the ammunition, provisions, and forage **stores** shown by icons. The player's observation agrees: an independent HQ is selectable on the campaign map, while a subordinate brigade does not show its own campaign supply panel there.

The installed game's `CampaignArmyPanel.UpdateStats` reads `Regiment.supplystate`, multiplies it by 100, and places the result in `supplytext`. `CampaignArmyPanel.SetIcons` separately reads `Regiment.groupsupplystate[0..3]` to set the ammunition, provisions, and forage icons. The game writer saves the HQ's `groupsupplystate[0..3] × 100` at `groups.dat` offsets 6–9. The `paths.dat` additional-unit record saves `supplystate` in its count-dependent five-value supply header, after supply speed, latest reinforcements, and reinforcements needed. Thus the panel's main flow percentage and its carried-store icons have **different saved sources**.

## Stock and hierarchy

The game serializes four `supplyinstock` values in each fully matched combat-unit `paths.dat` record. The six combat units imported in the controlled creation test all have game-written stock and unique four-value path identities; [the audit](../scripts/Audit-NewUnitSupply.ps1) reproduces those matches. `BattleUnits.CheckTimeIssues` calls `ResupplyAllUnits`, which reads and writes the stock on live `Regiment` objects; `Regiment.Update` calls `UpdateIIPSupplyLink`, which also reads and writes stock. `Regiment.UpdateSupplyIcon` reads individual stock. This establishes per-unit stock **in campaign state**, even when the campaign UI does not offer a separate brigade panel. These call references can be reproduced with [`Find-GameMethodCallers.ps1`](../scripts/Find-GameMethodCallers.ps1).

`Regiment.UpdateGroupStrength` iterates attached units. For provisions and forage it accumulates each subordinate's `supplyinstock` divided by that unit's strength, sick, and losses, then normalizes the group result by contributing-unit counts. It also handles ammunition and other branch-specific ratios. `BattleUnits.UpdateCampaignSupplyStates` checks `istopsupplyunit` and uses a top supply unit's own `supplyinstock`, group strength, and `GamePrefs.groupsupplyreserve` to update `groupsupplystate`. This supports an **HQ plus subordinates** model: command records have their own supply fields, while group values can summarize subordinate stores and be recalculated. The exact update order, which HQs act as top supply units, and redistribution amounts are not yet established. The I. CORPS path stock in these tests stayed `(10,10,10,10)` while its group stores changed; this alone does not show that it was the source of the infantry unit's replenishment. Do not treat `groups.dat` offsets 6–9 as durable user inputs.

**Interpretation of the displayed value:** The HQ panel's main `Supplies` number is the selected command's own `supplystate` (incoming flow), not the arithmetic mean, median, or mode of subordinate combat-unit stock. The **store icons** are the values that can summarize attached units. In `UpdateGroupStrength`, at least the provisions and forage paths add each eligible unit's normalized stock and divide by a contributing-unit count, which is a mean of category ratios. The ammunition branches differ, and top supply units can receive separate reserve calculations; no single universal aggregate applies to every icon or native tier. The paired Test 6 saves illustrate this distinction: the infantry stock was halved, but its flow remained `1`; the parent division's group-store values changed, while the I. CORPS main flow stayed `1`.

**Parent proximity and transfers:** The battlefield `Regiment.CalculateSupplyLine` does consider a parent, distance, and obstruction. The campaign `Regiment.UpdateIIPSupplyLink` instead traces nearby IIPs, supply depots, transport capacity, distance, and inherited perks. The currently inspected campaign code does not prove that a nearby higher HQ directly funnels stock to a lower HQ; the exact redistribution path remains unknown. ADC's existing reparent edit changes a combat record's `Parent_ID` and does not patch that unit's `paths.dat` stock. Since the game loads additional path data by the four-value unit identity, the same unit's stock should remain associated with it immediately after a reparent. A controlled game transfer/resave is still required to confirm that stock retention and any subsequent redistribution in play.

`Regiment.Initialize` creates a four-value `supplyinstock` array equal to the unit's current strength in every slot when stock is null or empty. `BattleUnits.CreateUnitDefault` also assigns strength-scaled starting stock to a newly created combat unit, with branch-specific indexes. In the controlled import route, new units had no added path record, and the game's next save wrote stocks close to their initial strengths after a small amount of campaign consumption. This identifies a game-generated **full-stock initial default** for that route. [Test 6](SUPPLY_STOCK_EXPERIMENT.md) later validated a reduced stock edit on an existing unit, but not on a newly created unit.

## Paired-save calculation for I. CORPS artillery stores

The read-only [artillery audit](../scripts/Audit-I-Corps-ArtillerySupply.ps1) resolves I. CORPS by its saved command identity, follows saved parent IDs to all descendants, matches artillery units to `paths.dat` by game identity, and calculates the mean of each attached artillery unit's `supplyinstock[1] / (total men + wounded) × 100`. It found 11 artillery units under 21 descendant groups, with unchanged unit counts and strengths in each pair. The calculated mean matches the I. CORPS `groups.dat` artillery-store percentage to its saved five-decimal precision:

| Game-written saves | I. CORPS artillery store | Calculated mean of 11 subordinate units | I. CORPS own `paths.dat` stock |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test 6 paused → unpaused | 78.93917 → 81.1623 | 78.939162858 → 81.162301632 | `(10,10,10,10)` → `(10,10,10,10)` |
| Test 8 paused → unpaused | 78.93917 → 79.50203 | 78.939162858 → 79.502030586 | `(10,10,10,10)` → `(10,10,10,10)` |

All 11 subordinate artillery stock values changed in both pairs. Their index-1 stock sums rose from 516.5581 to 530.71541 in Test 6 and to 520.13816 in Test 8. This establishes that **this I. CORPS artillery-store figure is an arithmetic mean of its subordinate artillery units' normalized stores**, rather than a rise in the HQ's own saved stock. It does not establish the source of replenishment or the calculation for the HQ's separate incoming-flow percentage. Other store categories and command types still need their own checks.

## Controlled next test

[Test 6](SUPPLY_STOCK_EXPERIMENT.md) changed one existing infantry unit's stock and obtained paused and time-advanced game resaves. The paused save retained the exact reduced stock; the later save showed partial replenishment and changed division/HQ group-store values. [Test 8](HQ_SUPPLY_FLOW_EXPERIMENT.md) changed only the matched `I. CORPS` `paths.dat` `supplystate` from 1 to 0.5. Its paused resave retained `0.5`, and the HQ panel returned to 100% almost immediately after time advanced; the next game save stored `1`. Together these tests establish that incoming flow is recalculated while individual stocks are accepted, consumed, and replenished in campaign time. They do not establish a single editable supply percentage for a newly created unit or a complete distribution formula.
